Technical Documentation

Forecasting Methodology

40,000 Correlated Trials

1. Polling & Fundamentals Blend (85 / 15)

For polled races, the expected margin μ_s weights recency-decayed, screen-adjusted polling composites at 85% and structural fundamentals at 15%:

μ_s = 0.85 · Margin_poll + 0.15 · Margin_fund

Fundamentals combine state Voter Index, the national Generic Ballot (D+8.8% × Elasticity), strict incumbency (±2.5% only when a sitting Senator runs; 0.0% in open seats), and candidate office tiers.

2. Cycle-Weighted Voter Index (0.65 / 0.35)

Each state's baseline Partisan Voter Index weights the 2024 presidential two-party margin deviation at 65% and the 2020 deviation at 35%:

PVI_s = 0.65 · Dev_2024 + 0.35 · Dev_2020

Registered Voter (RV) and Adult (A) samples are adjusted dynamically using an exponential saturation dampener exp(-S_total / 150) and bounded spending turnout skew.

3. Cholesky Covariance Matrix

Forecast errors correlate across state and regional lines. We construct a 35×35 covariance matrix Σ combining three orthogonal variance components:

  • National Wave Error: σ_nat = 2.0%
  • Regional Cluster Error: σ_reg = 1.5%
  • Idiosyncratic Noise: σ_idio = 2.5% (incumbents) to 4.0% (open seats)

Using Cholesky decomposition (Σ = L·Lᵀ), we draw 40,000 correlated multivariate normal outcomes Y = μ + L·Z.

4. Statutory RCV & Runoff Rules

Georgia Majority Rule: Requires >50.0% to win outright. Simulated trials where |Margin| < 2.5% advance to a December runoff.

Alaska & Maine RCV: Round 1 major-party shares are scaled by first_choice_share_major (0.94 in AK, 0.98 in ME). If neither crosses 50%, non-exhausted minor ballots transfer via empirical second-choice flows.

5. Independent Challengers & 3-Way Splits

Where Democrats did not field a nominee (Nebraska with Dan Osborn, Idaho with Todd Achilles, South Dakota with Brian Bengs), the Independent is modeled as the primary non-GOP challenger.

In Montana, where Independent Seth Bodnar runs alongside a Democratic nominee, a 7.0% coalition split deduction accounts for ballot-line division.

6. Non-Contested Chamber Baseline

Of the 100 U.S. Senate seats, 65 seats are not up for election in 2026: 34 held by Democrats/caucusing Independents and 31 held by Republicans.

Democrats and caucusing Independents must win at least 17 of the 35 contested seats (reaching 51 total) to gain an outright Senate majority.
