Model Architecture & Technical Specification

2026 U.S. Senate Forecasting Methodology

v2.6 Cross-Sectional Engine
1 85% Polling / 15% Fundamentals

For every polled race, the expected margin μ_s blends time-decayed, voter-screen-adjusted polling composites (85% weight) with structural race fundamentals (15% weight):

μ_s = 0.85 · Margin_poll + 0.15 · Margin_fund

Structural fundamentals (Margin_fund) combine state PVI, national Generic Ballot (D+8.8% × Elasticity), strict incumbency (±2.5% only if a sitting Senator is running; 0.0% in open seats), candidate office tiers, and controversy deductions.

2 0.65 / 0.35 Partisan Voter Index

Rather than an equal 50/50 split, each state's baseline Partisan Voter Index (PVI) weights the 2024 presidential two-party margin deviation at 65% and the 2020 deviation at 35%:

PVI_s = 0.65 · Dev_2024 + 0.35 · Dev_2020

Registered Voter (RV) and Adult (A) polls are dynamically adjusted using an exponential spending saturation dampener exp(-S_total / 150) and a bounded relative spending turnout skew 0.5 · tanh(0.5 · ln(S_D / S_R)).

3 Cholesky Covariance & 40k Trials

Election errors are correlated across states. We construct a 35×35 covariance matrix Σ combining three orthogonal variance components:

  • National Wave Error: σ_nat = 2.0%
  • Regional Cluster Error: σ_reg = 1.5%
  • Idiosyncratic Noise: σ_idio = 2.5% (incumbents) to 4.0% (open seats)

Using Cholesky decomposition (Σ = L·Lᵀ), we draw 40,000 correlated multivariate normal outcome vectors Y = μ + L·Z.

4 RCV (AK, ME) & GA Runoff Rules

Georgia Majority Rule: Requires >50.0% to win outright. Accounting for minor third-party vote share, simulated trials where |Margin| < 2.5% are flagged as advancing to a December runoff.

Alaska & Maine RCV: First-choice major candidate vote shares are scaled by first_choice_share_major (0.94 in AK, 0.98 in ME). If neither crosses 50% in Round 1, non-exhausted minor ballots transfer according to empirical preference flows (75% GOP flow in AK Top-4; 55% Dem flow in ME).

5 Independent Challenger Modeling

In states where Democrats stood down or lack a major contender (Nebraska with Dan Osborn, Idaho with Todd Achilles, South Dakota with Brian Bengs), the Independent is modeled as the primary non-GOP challenger and rendered in a 4-tier purple palette when leading.

In Montana, where Independent Seth Bodnar runs alongside a Democratic nominee on the ballot, a calibrated 7.0% coalition split penalty accounts for ballot-line leakage.

6 34D – 31R Non-Contested Baseline

Of the 100 U.S. Senate seats, 65 seats are not up for election in 2026: 34 held by Democrats/caucusing Independents and 31 held by Republicans.

To win outright Senate control (51 seats), Democrats/Independents must win at least 17 of the 35 contested seats (or 16 for a 50–50 chamber split), while Republicans need 20 of the 35 contested seats to reach 51.
